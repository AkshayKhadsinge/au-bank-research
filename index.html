01

Business, model and competitive position

AU operates as a scheduled small finance bank. It amalgamated Fincare SFB effective 1-Apr-2024 (pooling of interests, 7.35 cr shares issued). RBI granted in-principle approval to become a universal bank on 7-Aug-2025, and the bank filed its final application in March 2026. AU reports standalone results as a single bank with no material subsidiaries, so this note uses standalone audited figures throughout.

Funding — Deposits were ₹1,52,661 cr at Mar-26, with CASA of ₹43,357 cr (28%). CASA, retail term deposits and non-callable bulk term deposits together make up 79% of deposits. The balance-sheet CD ratio is 88% (80% excluding refinance, per company). Average LCR was 119% in Q4FY26. FY26 cost of funds was 6.75% (company definition), ending the year at 6.49% in Q4 against a 5.25% repo rate cited on the call.

Competitive position — Among listed SFBs in the peer set, AU has about 3.3x the deposits of the next largest (Equitas). It also has the highest FY26 ROE (14.2%) and the lowest GNPA (2.0%). As a universal bank, it would compete with mid-size private banks such as Federal and City Union, which earn 11–13% ROE and trade at ~1.8x book — so the SFB and private peer groups are shown separately throughout.

What the history shows — NII compounded 31% a year over FY21-FY26 and PAT 18%. Growth came from branch-led expansion and the Fincare merger in FY25. Returns fell from 16.4% ROE in FY22 to 13.0–14.2% in FY24-FY26 for three reasons: higher funding costs after the 2022-24 rate cycle (model cost of funds rose from 5.3% to 7.1%); the FY24-FY25 investment phase, which took cost-to-income to ~64% in FY24; and FY25 stress in MFI and credit cards, which pushed provisions to ₹1,793 cr.

Non-comparable periods: FY21 other income includes a one-off gain on the Aavas stake sale. FY24 carries ₹76.8 cr of merger expenses as an exceptional item. FY25 ratios on average balances are overstated, since pooling brings Fincare's income in for the full year while opening balances exclude it (model FY25 ROE is 14.2% against 13.1% reported).

03

Asset quality, provisioning, capital, liquidity

Asset quality — GNPA fell from 4.25% (FY21, Covid) to 1.66–1.67% in FY23-FY24, rose to 2.28% in FY25 on MFI and card stress, then improved to 2.03% at Mar-26. NNPA was 0.74% in both years. Specific coverage (1 − NNPA/GNPA) is 64%; the company's headline PCR of 85% includes technical write-offs. Q4FY26 gross slippages were ~₹659 cr. Management says 92% of the MFI book is covered by the CGFMU guarantee scheme.

Credit cost — 1.34% of average advances in FY26, after 1.99% in FY25 (merger-distorted denominator), against 0.30–0.67% in FY23-FY24.

Capital — As an SFB, AU holds capital only against credit risk (Basel II, standardised), with a 15% minimum CRAR and 7.5% minimum Tier 1. At Mar-26, CET1 (all Tier 1) was 16.85% and CRAR 18.68%, on RWA of ₹1,16,234 cr — 61% of assets. CRAR has fallen from 23.6% (FY23) as RWA grew 29% in FY26, faster than equity (+16%). At its 27-Apr-2026 meeting, the board sought shareholder approval for enabling resolutions to raise up to ₹7,500 cr of equity and ₹6,000 cr of debt (incl. ₹2,500 cr Tier 2).

This is an enabling authority, not a committed raise, and the base case assumes no new equity. Moving to universal-bank status should lower the minimum CRAR to 11.5% including CCB, but adds operational (and potentially market) risk RWA — modelled as 12.5 × 15% × three-year average gross income from FY28, which takes base CET1 from 16.5% (FY27E) to 14.6% (FY28E).

Liquidity & concentration — Cash and bank balances were 5.6% of deposits and investments 29% of deposits at Mar-26. Average LCR was 119%. Top-20 depositor/borrower concentration, geographic mix, CA/SA split and maturity profile are published in the annual report and Pillar 3 schedules; the FY26 annual report was released after the cut-off, so these are not analysed here. On segment concentration alone, 66% of GLP is retail secured and unsecured (MFI plus digital) is 7.2%.

04

Investment thesis: evidence for and against

Four testable claims underpin the base case. Each is weighed against the strongest evidence on both sides, and tied to the specific model driver it depends on.

1. Loans can compound ~17–19% p.a. for five years, funded by deposits
Supporting

GLP +21%, deposits +23% in FY26; touchpoints +334 in FY26 to 2,790; management targets 2–2.5x nominal GDP growth.

Challenging

CASA ratio fell 38% → 28% (FY23-FY26); base case needs deposit growth ~20% FY27E, ~1.6x system deposit growth of 12.2% — i.e. continued share gains.

Model driver: segment growth · CD ratio 88% · CASA 28.5–30.5%
2. ROE can rise to ~16–17% through efficiency and normal credit costs
Supporting

Q4FY26 ROE 17.0%, ROA 1.8%; management guides cost/assets below 4% in FY27, ~3.5% in 3-5 years, credit cost ~90bp of assets, ROA ~1.8% aspiration.

Challenging

FY26 ROE was 14.2%; NIM fell 29bp in FY26; mix is shifting toward Commercial Banking (10.4% yield); management says Q4 seasonality will not repeat.

Model driver: yield 12.85% → 12.75% · CoF 6.10% → 6.05% · opex/non-cash assets 4.15% → 3.82% · credit cost 1.20% of GLP
3. Capital supports growth through the universal-bank transition without dilution
Supporting

CET1 16.9%; base-case internal capital generation 17-19% a year vs RWA growth 16-20% (excl. transition step).

Challenging

CRAR down ~490bp since FY23; ₹7,500 cr equity enabling resolution in place; assumed operational-risk RWA on transition cuts CET1 by ~2pp.

Model driver: RWA density 61% · op-risk RWA from FY28 · Tier 2 = 10.8% of Tier 1
4. The market price already reflects claims 1–3
Supporting

P/B 3.7x vs peer medians 0.85x (SFB) and 1.31x (private); P/E 28x FY26.

Challenging

AU already has the best return and asset-quality record among SFBs; universal-bank optionality is hard to model and may not be priced in at all.

Model driver: CoE 13.34% · terminal ROE 15% · terminal g 7%

On the evidence, claims 1–3 are plausible and form the base case. Claim 1 is the most fragile, on funding cost and CASA. Claim 4 is the analyst's own judgement: even at ~17% ROE for five years, the price needs a much longer high-return period, or a much lower discount rate, than the base case assumes.

Is growth supportable? Funding: base deposits must grow 20% in FY27E, easing to 15% by FY31E — ~1.6x the 12.2% system rate, implying AU's deposit share keeps rising. It has done this every year since FY21, but it is the assumption most exposed to competition. No base or downside run requires new equity — the ₹7,500 cr enabling resolution is a relevant option, not a necessity. Regulation: the analysis assumes the universal-bank minima per RBI's 26-Apr-2024 transition guidelines.

06

Valuation

Method. For a bank, deposits and loans are operating activities, so this note does not use free-cash-flow or working-capital DCF. The primary method is a three-stage residual-income (RI) model: value = book value at 31-Mar-2026 + PV of (net income − k × opening book value). Stage 1 (FY27E-FY31E) comes from the integrated model. In stage 2 (FY32-FY41), ROE fades linearly from the FY31E level (18.8% on opening book) to a terminal 15%, and book growth fades to 7%. A continuing value follows. Value at 31-Mar-2026 is rolled forward 59 days at k to the valuation date and divided by 75.66 cr diluted shares (period-end shares plus the 1.1% ESOP dilution implied by FY26 basic vs diluted EPS).

Cost of equity 13.34% = risk-free 7.20% (10-year point interpolated from RBI auction cut-offs of 7.07% for 7 years and 7.34% for 14 years, late May 2026) + beta 1.02 (two years of weekly returns vs NIFTY 50) × ERP 6.0% (analyst assumption).

Reading the peer table. Multiples are not averaged mechanically. SFB peers trade at 0.8-1.6x book on single-digit or negative ROE after MFI losses. Profitable private banks trade at 1.3-1.8x on 11-13% ROE. AU's 3.7x is 2.1x the P/B the peer P/B-ROE fit implies at its ROE. The market is paying for growth duration and universal-bank optionality that one year of cross-section data cannot capture.

Credit

MFI and unsecured books (7.2% of GLP, 25% and 14% yields) proved volatile in FY25. Vehicle and micro-business loans are cyclical; the downside case cuts FY27E ROE to 4.8%.

Funding

CASA fell 10pp in three years, and deposit growth at ~1.6x the system rate raises the cost of funds. Every +10bp on cost of funds costs roughly ₹180 cr of FY27E pre-tax profit.

Regulatory

Timing of the universal-bank licence is uncertain; capital treatment (operational/market risk), CRR/SLR and priority-sector rules would change. The op-risk RWA proxy used here is an assumption.

Execution & governance

CFO transition (interim CFO from Sept-2025; new CFO appointed 27-Apr-2026); ESOP cost is rising (reserve ₹124 cr to ₹241 cr in FY26); Fincare CBS migration completed per management.

Valuation

At 3.7x book, the shares are sensitive to any change in the growth-duration narrative. Conversely, if AU sustains ~19% ROE for longer than the fade assumes, fair value approaches the price.

Catalysts to monitor — the final universal-bank licence and the capital rules that apply; quarterly cost-of-funds and CASA trajectory; credit cost against the ~90bp guidance; any use of the ₹7,500 cr equity authority; the FY26 annual report (concentration, maturity and CA/SA detail, published after this note's cut-off).

Conclusion. AU's filed numbers show a well-capitalised, growing and profitable franchise whose returns are recovering after the FY25 credit cycle. The integrated model funds and capitalises ~17% loan growth at ROE rising to ~17% without new equity. Even so, the base-case residual-income value is ₹527 per share, with ₹396–1,188 across reasonable cost-of-equity and terminal-growth assumptions, ₹299–1,317 across cost-of-equity and terminal-ROE assumptions, and ₹261–883 across scenarios. The gap is a statement about assumptions, not a verdict: the price is consistent with the model only if AU sustains ROE of ~19% for decades at a cost of equity near 12.5%, or earns the base-case ROE at a CoE below 10%. Readers should form their own view on those two inputs. This is not a recommendation.

Limitations. Interest expense is not split between deposits and borrowings before the annual report, so a mixed cost of funds is modelled.
